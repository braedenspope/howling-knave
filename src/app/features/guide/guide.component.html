How Training Works

The crew of the Howling Knave will teach you what they know — but only if you put in the hours. Here is how the work pays off.

schedule

Your day is eight hours

Each day on the board is eight one-hour blocks (sleep is assumed). Every day, two of those hours are ship duties — the work that keeps you afloat — and they land at random. Whatever is left is yours to spend training with the crew.

You choose how long to train

Pick a training, then pick how much of your day to give it. A longer block costs more hours, but moves you further. Go all in on one crewmate, or split the day between several — it's your call.

Earn points, one hour at a time

Training is run hour by hour. Every hour of a block is its own roll — the DM calls for whichever roll fits what you're doing — and every roll that lands is worth one point. A longer block is worth more because you sit through more of it, not because it counts for extra.

A wasted day is never wasted

Put two hours or more into a training on a given day and miss every roll, and you still walk away with 1 point for the attempt. You can have a terrible day at the mast, but you can't have an empty one.

Plan your crossing

Not every roll lands, so a 12-point training usually takes more than twelve hours — most of a short crossing if you give it your full attention. When you add a block, the board shows how many points you've banked, how many you still need, and how many hours you've already planned.

info Try not to plan past the goal. If you master a training partway through a block, the rest of that block is free time — you'll describe at the table what you do with it.
